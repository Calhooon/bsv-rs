**The rule you carry** (the owner's, in the stack's matrix rows 26 and 30 and `lean/Sdk.lean`, relayed by the keeper): busy is the body's word, never the HTTP status; a 502, 503 or 504 without it, a connection error or a timeout is a transport fault, retryable, with no promise about what the far side did. On a broadcaster the fault says whether the request was sent: a failure before any byte left is `neverSent` (the caller may release its inputs), one after the body was written, a timeout included, is `mayHaveBeenReceived` (the caller holds them; once written, no trace ends `neverSent`). A 429, and a 503 carrying `Retry-After`, is `rateLimited` with its wait whatever the body is; a rate limit is never a verdict on the transaction. A chain tracker's provider error is an error, never a verdict (no `false`).
